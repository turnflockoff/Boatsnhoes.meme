Two kinds of GPU work

What's real today, what's later.

Now · being built and tested

Useful AI jobs

A coordinator hands out small, signed AI jobs (for example: answer a prompt with a pinned open model). A worker program on your GPU runs them and sends the result back, and the coordinator checks the work. The software exists and has thousands of automated tests; it has not yet run on a real GPU.

If a $BNH chain is ever launched, the proposal is proof-of-work mining with a memory-hungry algorithm that favours regular GPUs and CPUs over specialised machines. The chain, the algorithm, and how it would connect to useful work are all still open.

How the test network works

Checked before it counts.

A result only counts after it's been checked. Some checks are hidden, so a rig can't behave well only when it's being watched.

  1. Join with a one-time codeThe operator gives each rig a single-use code. The rig gets its own key; keys are stored only as fingerprints, and a rig can be switched off at any time.
  2. Get a signed jobEach job is signed by the coordinator, names the exact model file by its fingerprint, and has a deadline. Workers refuse anything unsigned or changed.
  3. Run it on one cardThe worker runs the pinned open model locally (llama.cpp with Vulkan is the first candidate), one worker per card, without admin rights.
  4. Send the answer backWith a receipt that must match the job's version, input and model fingerprints.
  5. Get checkedSome results are quietly re-run by another, randomly picked rig; some jobs are hidden tests with a known answer. Disagreements go to a third rig or to a person.
  6. Points, simulatedChecked work is tallied as off-chain points in a signed, public report. Points are bookkeeping for the test. They are not coins and aren't paid out.
Hardware

Starting small, on purpose.

Test rig

Two Radeon RX 470s

Reported setup: Ryzen 5 5600X, 32 GB RAM, Ubuntu, one 4 GB and one 8 GB card. Older cards that AMD's main AI toolkit doesn't support, which is exactly why they're a good test.

What they can do

Small AI models

The first job type is text generation with a small open model (Qwen2.5 0.5B, about 400 MB, Apache-2.0). Training big models or heavy rendering isn't realistic on these cards.

Your card

Check it in 10 seconds

The contribute page reads what your browser reports about your graphics card, locally, and lets you sign up as a tester. Nothing is installed.

  • Running a GPU costs electricity. In the test, that cost is yours and nothing pays it back.
  • Never run a "miner" from a DM, a search ad or a file-sharing site. Real downloads will be signed by at least two people and linked only from this site and GitHub (how to check).
The honest bit

Why useful work, not just hashing.

Mining costs money, but cost alone doesn't make anything valuable. Electricity spent solving puzzles is gone either way. What mining does well is spread coins fairly and make attacking a chain expensive.

Useful

Work someone wants

An answered prompt or a sorted document is useful whether or not a coin ever exists. That's the bet: GPUs doing work people actually need.

Fair

Anyone with a GPU

No presale and no insiders. If coins are ever issued, the plan is that they come from work, starting at the first block.

Honest

Nothing promised

No price, profit or payout talk. If it doesn't work on real cards, the logbook will say so.

Straight answers.

Can I mine $BNH now?

No. There's no chain to mine. You can help test the GPU side by signing up on the contribute page.

Will testers earn coins later?

No promise of that. Test points are simulated and only exist to prove the checking and bookkeeping work.

Can I rent out my GPU through this?

Not yet. Renting compute to outside customers would need payments, legal review and a safe way to run strangers' jobs on your machine. None of that exists. Today the network only runs job types the project defines.

NVIDIA, Apple or Intel graphics?

The first test is AMD on Linux with Vulkan. Other cards may work later; sign up and tell us what you have.

Where can I see the results?

Signed reports will appear in the public logbook once rigs run real jobs. All code is on GitHub.
