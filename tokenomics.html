The supply plan.

If a $BNH coin is ever launched, this is how its supply is proposed to work. Every number on this page can still change, and the hardware test comes first.

No coin exists. There's no token, chain, presale or airdrop, and nothing is for sale. This page describes a design. It is not an offer and says nothing about what anything will be worth.
The proposed numbers

Four numbers make the plan.

Each one has a status. "Proposed" means it's the working plan; "leaning" means it's likely but not locked; nothing here is "final" yet.

Block time

60 seconds

A new block about once a minute, 1,440 a day. Difficulty would adjust every block so the pace holds as miners come and go. Proposed

Halving

Every 3.5 years

The block reward halves every 1,840,860 blocks (3.5 years of 60-second blocks), so new supply shrinks on a fixed, public schedule. Proposed

Starting reward

27 per block

The first era pays 27 coins per block, about 38,880 a day, split among whoever mines those blocks. Leaning

Total supply

About 99.4 million

27 × 1,840,860 × 2 = 99,406,440: the most that could ever exist with a strict cap. Whether to cap at all is still open (see below). Leaning

Emission schedule

Half in 3.5 years, then slower.

With these numbers, 50% of the supply would be mined in the first 3.5 years and 75% by year 7. That front-loading is a trade-off: it rewards early miners heavily, and it's one of the things still being weighed.

How coins would start

Mined from block one. Sold by no one.

The plan leans towards a fair launch: every coin comes from mining, starting at the first block, with nothing set aside for the team or sold early.

Leaning: fair launch, no premine

Why

No insiders holding a pile from day one, nothing to "rug", and no presale to sell. It's the most honest start a mined coin can have.

The catch

Who's ready first

A fair launch still favours whoever shows up first with the most hardware (scripts, rented GPUs, botnets), and it leaves no money for audits or lawyers. The launch plan has to deal with both.

  • No presale, private round, whitelist or airdrop is planned. If you see one, it isn't us (Stay safe).
  • Points from the current GPU test are simulated bookkeeping. They are not coins, and there is no plan or promise to turn them into coins.
  • Before any launch: chain choice, a public testnet, an independent security review and a legal review.

Straight answers.

Can I buy $BNH?

No. Nothing exists to buy. Any $BNH for sale today belongs to someone else using the name.

Why 27?

27 per block with a 3.5-year halving lands just under 100 million total, a round, readable cap. It also fits the meme. It's a proposal, not a magic number.

What would a coin be worth?

We don't say, and nobody can know. Value would come from whether people want to use and hold it, not from this table. Most new coins lose value.

Do GPU testers get coins?

No. The test network keeps simulated points to check that the bookkeeping works. They are not coins, and there's no promise they ever will be.

When will this be decided?

After the hardware test on real GPUs, and after legal advice. The changes will be posted here and on GitHub.
